# Beck — implementation plan

One language for the frontend, the backend, the database, the container, and the cluster — where
every tier is a pure function or a fold, and time enters at declared merge points.

## Provenance

The plan derives from George's original conversation, preserved in
[`00-original-idea.md`](00-original-idea.md) (the share link wasn't machine-readable; the transcript
was supplied directly and is now the source of truth). A first draft of this plan was written before
the transcript was available; checking it against the original changed three load-bearing things:

- **The data tier is event-sourced, not relational-first**: the database is a `durable` fold over
  an event stream; queries are pure functions of that state, kept incremental by the compiler.
  Relational storage is a *substrate* (read models in Postgres), not the semantic model.
- **The browser is a patch interpreter by default** (~KBs of compiler residue), with a per-component
  upgrade to client-side rendering — not a fat-WASM-client-first design.
- **Deploys ride the stream**: typed `migrate`/`upcast` functions demanded at deploy, drain/resume
  choreography, replay as a feature.

## The three questions from the brief, answered

| Question | Short answer | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Python-like without losing the Lisp power? | **Yes — solved problem.** Homoiconicity is a property of the core AST, not the notation (Elixir/Julia/Nim prove it). One canonical `Node` AST, two faithful surfaces; the missing Python piece is a block-passing call form so macros receive indented bodies. The S-expression surface stays as the spec and macro-debugging notation | [`02`](02-syntax.md) |
| Dependencies best-in-class *and* open source? | Rust host; Salsa; dual Cranelift/LLVM codegen; thin-client first, Wasmtime for server-side WASM; **Postgres as durable substrate + MIT-licensed differential dataflow for incremental views** (Materialize-class capability without the BUSL licence); DataFusion for analytics; apko for reproducible daemonless images; kube-rs; Crossplane (Terraform is BUSL — excluded). Licences, benchmarks, swap costs per dependency | [`07`](07-dependencies.md) |
| Kubernetes under the hood? | Yes — as a **compiler backend** behind a `Platform` trait, never as language semantics. Infra is derived from program analysis: a `durable` fold ⇒ volume + snapshots; `merge_clients()` ⇒ websocket ingress; effect rows ⇒ least-privilege NetworkPolicy/RBAC/grants. `beck run` needs no cluster, container, or registry | [`06`](06-kubernetes-and-packaging.md) |

## Documents

Three kinds of document, in one table. **Design** documents say what Beck is and are corrected in
place when they stop being true. **Reports** are chapters about a phase or a subsystem, each ending
with what it does *not* establish. **Surveys** are dated and keep their date, because a survey's
value is the evidence for a verdict rather than the verdict.

Every row is three sentences. What a chapter established, what it refused to claim, and where the
argument is — the chapters themselves are where the detail lives.

| 99 | [The data tier's means of combination](99-the-data-tier-means-of-combination.md) | **Every row of the relational basis is built, and the read model's SQL reaches all of it: the join, both of its indexes, all four aggregates, the difference and `distinct`.** SICP's test applied to the view algebra rather than the language: primitives sound, means of abstraction sound, **means of combination unary at the time** — every operator took one collection, so a program relating two of them escaped into a per-element function that captured the accumulator and was reapplied on every event. §99.6's inference closes that with **no edit to any program and no syntax**, for a lookup and for a filtered group alike: `for x in xs:` whose body asks `map_get(ys, k(x))` compiles to a `Join` over an index, maintained from both sides, and §99.9's gate reports 19 units of maintenance at 200 rows and at 1,600 against 415 and 3,215 with the operator switched off; a body that filters `ys` by an equality against `x` compiles to `arrange_by` and a join answering with the group, which is 4.5–4.9× less work per event on `examples/board.beck` and **1.1× when one group holds the whole collection** — the operator removes the scan and leaves the group. §99.9 item 6 then takes the group for the one question the language can already spell: `list_len` over that filter is answered from a tally the join keeps, so `corpus/35-workload.beck` copies **one** entry out of an arrangement at 200 issues and at 1,600 against 202 and 1,602. `min` and `max` per group followed once `list_min` and `list_max` became primitives, and they **cost the same as each other**, which §99.9 predicted they would not: the asymmetry it forecast — a `BTreeMap` prefix range is entered from its start and not its end, and Beck has no descending order to key by — is real of a range over an index of the *rows* and dissolves under the count's own rule, that an aggregate is the reading operator's and not the index's, because a tree an operator builds itself is bounded at both ends. `Op::GroupBy` holds one multiset per group, `corpus/36-auction.beck` costs **72 backend steps at 200 bids and at 1,600 against 4,097 and 32,097** with it switched off, and a bid between the standing ends publishes nothing at all. `sum` was the one that owed a decision rather than an operator, and both of the edges it named — a `Float` total maintained by subtraction is not the number a left fold produces, and an `Int` one reaches intermediate values `checked_add` can raise on — turned out to be edges of the **fold** rather than of the sum: **a sum is its answer, not the order it was added in**, so `list_sum` is the exact total, raises only when *that* leaves `Int`, and is a conservative extension of `+` — the same answer wherever the fold has one, and an answer where the fold overflows on the way back inside. `Float` gets none, because there the same definition would *disagree* with the fold rather than extend it. `Agg::Sum` therefore keeps a running total and no multiset, and `corpus/37-ledger.beck` costs **47 backend steps at 200 postings and at 1,600 against 2,060 and 16,060** with it switched off; the aggregate publishes a total no `Int` holds instead of raising one, so a group with no reader cannot fail a render the recompute completes. The **difference** and the intersection by key came last and cost least, because `map_contains` was already a primitive where every operator before it had waited on a spelling: `filter_list(xs, lambda x: not map_contains(m, k(x)))` is `Op::Restrict`, the one binary operator whose *output is one of its inputs* — which is what §99.5 decision 2's "no representational change at all" turned out to mean, and why a filter can have it where a join's element being a row says a filter may not have a join. `corpus/38-backorders.beck` costs **134 backend steps at 200 orders and at 1,600 against 10,064 and 80,064** with it switched off, measured on a delivery because the left-hand half never cost anything. **`distinct` closed the table, and what stood in its way was a name.** The arrangement it needs had existed since the extremes — `Op::GroupBy`'s multiset is a count per distinct value — while nothing in the language *named* the question: both of the library's duplicate-dropping functions are folds, and a fold is one opaque operator. `list_unique` is the name, and it is `unique`'s answer rather than a third one, so no program has two functions for one question; `Op::Distinct` publishes each value at the input key of its first occurrence, which makes the output a sub-order of the input's and is why nothing downstream had to learn anything. `corpus/39-topics.beck` costs **62 backend steps at 200 notes and at 1,600 against 2,280 and 17,680** for the same program with the dedup written as a fold, and the one defect it had — a value leaving a position another value is arriving at — was found by the corpus-wide differential and is gated by a written log. §99.2 argues the gap was an oversight rather than a decision, on the evidence that this project refuses things with paragraphs and joins have three "not built" rows and no reason anywhere; §99.3 measures it — 18 capture sites in 10 programs, three of which move per event, and `beck explain cost` prints the defect while its own tally excludes it. §99.8 is the second solver the join forces into existence and the ladder that makes its choices *correct* rather than merely deterministic: each rung deletes a constant instead of tuning one, and an estimate is needed **once per plan**, because the log makes the counterfactual exact. **And the SQL surface took the lot, which is what item 9 was for.** `select … join … on`, `group by` with all four aggregates and `select distinct` are answered by those same operators, because `beck-core/src/query.rs` joins nothing, groups nothing and deduplicates nothing — it writes the loop a person would have written and hands it to §99.6's recogniser, so there is one join in this project rather than two that agree by inspection. What it cost instead was two **agreements**: one rule for what a column *is* (`Table::row_values` — a newtype seen through, an `Option` flattened), because a join comparing `Id("p1")` with `"p1"` would answer no rows for two columns a person can see are equal; and a named refusal wherever SQL's equality is not `Value`'s — a nullable join key, or a join across two types. A join over two collections of 200 rows and of 1,600 costs **5,004 backend steps and 40,004** against **244,004 and 15,392,004** with the recognition switched off, which is the nested loop an interpreter would have been. |

## The one-paragraph version

`beck` is a statically typed, homoiconic language with a Python-like surface in which the original
`(my-javascript (my-css (my-html)))` becomes literal: the page is a pure function of state, state is
a durable fold over an event stream, and hand-written JavaScript disappears — it's compiler residue.
Clients propose typed commands; one declared merge point admits time and nondeterminism; everything
downstream is deterministic, so replay, time-travel debugging and optimistic UI come from the
semantics rather than from frameworks. Placement is typed and checkable (secrets provably cannot
reach the browser), views are kept incremental by the compiler, and `beck build` emits not a binary
but a deployable system — reproducible OCI images plus a Kubernetes object graph derived from the
program's own effects — which `beck deploy` applies with typed migrations gating the rollout.
