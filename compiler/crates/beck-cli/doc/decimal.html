`bignum`'s division with its failure translated into this module's vocabulary.

The divisor is non-zero at every call below, so the `Err` arm is unreachable — and writing it is what keeps this file's row a single error type. A library whose callers have to handle two unrelated error unions because of what it happens to be built on is a library leaking its implementation, and `try:` is the tool for not doing that (`docs/45`).

divide_to

divide_to(a: Decimal, b: Decimal, scale: Int, rule: Rounding) -> Decimal

defon anyraises(DecimalError)

`a / b` at exactly `scale` fractional digits, rounded by `rule`.

This is the division that always answers, and the caller has said what it wants: how many digits, and what to do with the one after them. `exact_quotient` is the one that refuses.

exact_quotient

exact_quotient(a: Decimal, b: Decimal) -> Decimal

defon anyraises(DecimalError)

`a / b`, exactly, or a refusal.

The digits are produced one at a time and the loop stops when the remainder reaches zero. A quotient that never gets there — a third — raises rather than rounding, and §56.3 is the argument: a `/` that silently picked a scale and a rounding rule would be *guessing*, and this library set already refuses to guess in the two places it costs the most (`money.beck`'s `split`, `docs/46` §46.2's parsers).

`divide_to` is the rounded division, and it takes both the scale and the rule.

max_scale

max_scale() -> Int

defon anyno effects

The most fractional digits `/` will produce before it refuses.

A bound rather than a proof: a terminating quotient needs as many digits as the larger of the twos and the fives in its reduced divisor, and deciding *that* needs a `gcd` and a factorisation this library does not have. Forty is past anything a program asks for — money is two places, an IEEE double is about seventeen significant digits — and a quotient that has not terminated in forty is one whose caller should be choosing a scale and a rule.

It is also what keeps a **refusal** cheap, and that is worth saying rather than hiding: the expansion costs one long division per digit, so the bound is the work done before `/` gives up. At a hundred it was twenty-five seconds on the divisor below, which is a gate nobody would keep. §56.6 records what the bound gets wrong: an exact quotient needing more than forty places.
