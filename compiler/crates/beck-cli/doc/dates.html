Date

model Date

A civil date: a year, a month in `1..12`, and a day in `1..days_in_month`.

Deliberately not an instant. An instant is a number of milliseconds and the host formats it; a *date* is what you add a month to, and adding a month to a number of milliseconds is the bug this type exists to not have.

Field
year: Int
month: Int
day: Int

Duration

type Duration = newtype[Int]

An elapsed length of time, in milliseconds.

Elapsed, not calendar: a `Duration` of 24 hours added to an instant is 24 hours later, which is not the same thing as "the next day" wherever a day is not 24 hours long. `add_days` is the calendar operation and it works on a `Date`; this works on an instant. Keeping them apart is the point of having two types.

CalendarError

union CalendarError
Variant
BadDate(text: Str)

A string that is not `YYYY-MM-DD`, or is that shape and not a date — `2023-02-30` is caught here rather than silently becoming the first of March.

NotADuration(op: Str)

A duration times a duration, or divided by one. Neither is a length of time: an hour times an hour is an hour-squared, and an hour divided by an hour is the number two. `Num` asks for four methods, so the two with no answer say so — `money.beck` refuses the same pair for the same reason.

add_months

add_months(d: Date, n: Int) -> Date

defon anyno effects

`n` months on, with the day clamped to the end of the month it lands in.

2024-01-31 plus one month is 2024-02-29, not 2024-03-02. There is no answer here that is right for every caller — a monthly billing date and a "30 days later" are different questions — so this is the one that keeps the *month* and says so, and a caller who wants the other writes `add_days`. Clamping also makes the operation lossy: adding a month and subtracting one does not always return where it started, and the test below says so rather than leaving it to be found.

days_from_civil

days_from_civil(year: Int, month: Int, day: Int) -> Int

defon anyno effects

Days since 1970-01-01 for a civil date, whether or not that date exists.

Total on purpose: `days_from_civil(2023, 2, 30)` is the day after the 28th, because the arithmetic has no opinion. `parse_date` is where a date is *validated*, and keeping the two apart is why the arithmetic can be used to write the validation.

defon anyraises(CalendarError)

`YYYY-MM-DD`, read back — and validated, which is the half the arithmetic deliberately has not.

It **raises** rather than returning a `Result`, which is the shape `docs/08` §8.5.3 trap 2 and `documents.beck` settled for the whole library: a caller who wants a `Result` writes `try:`, and a caller already inside something fallible writes nothing.

render_date

render_date(d: Date) -> Str

defon anyno effects

`YYYY-MM-DD`.

Four digits for the year, which is what ISO 8601 requires and what the host's `time_format` writes. A year outside `0..9999` is rendered as the number it is, and is outside what that notation can say — stated because a library that quietly writes a five-character year into a four-character field is worse than one that says which.

render_duration

render_duration(d: Duration) -> Str

defon anyno effects

ISO 8601: `PT1H30M`, `PT0.500S`, `-PT2M`.

Hours are the largest unit used, and that is deliberate rather than lazy. ISO 8601's `P1D` is a *calendar* day and a `Duration` is elapsed milliseconds, so writing `P1D` here would claim something this type does not know — see the type's own comment. Zero renders as `PT0S`, which the standard requires to have at least one component.
